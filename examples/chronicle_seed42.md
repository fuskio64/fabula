# A Record of Cold Hollow

Cold Hollow held 10 souls at the opening of this record, and it covers 384 days of them. Nothing in it was invented; it was only watched, and told.

20 times, someone in Cold Hollow went hungry rather than ask for help. Pride is its own kind of famine.

When the record closes, 12 favors in Cold Hollow had still not been returned. The village kept those accounts in no book, which is why none of them were ever lost.

One soul in Cold Hollow did not live through these years. A village carries its dead quietly, which is how the dead end up carrying the village.

4 bonds were made in Cold Hollow in this time. Most lasting things begin without anyone marking the moment.

2 children were born in Cold Hollow in this time.

By the close of this record, Cold Hollow counted more souls than it had at the opening.

## The Village

The winter was a hungry one. More than half the houses in Cold Hollow had lean pantries, and no one pretended otherwise.

Cold Hollow came through that summer with full stores. Those who remember the lean years never quite take it for granted.

## Tam and Orin: a debt unpaid

Tam, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was talkative and tireless. Everyone had come to Orin at least once. Orin was proud and idle.

It began in autumn. When Orin's stores ran low, Tam filled the basket without being asked. A year went by. By winter nothing had been resolved. Orin owed Tam the kind of debt nobody writes down, and paid it with a closed door. Nobody said anything. Everybody counted it. Not many days passed. The favor sat heavy on Orin. It is a hard thing to owe, and Orin took to avoiding Tam's eye. When summer came again, the story had not ended. It came to hard words between Tam and Orin, in the open street. A year went by. By autumn nothing had been resolved. It fell to Bryn — who had seen enough winters to know how grudges end — to sit them at one table. Something mended. And so the fighting went on, season after season; some things need a witness more than a resolution.

The peace that was made did not keep; some cracks want to stay open.

## Orin and Wren: an unfinished business

Wren, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was proud and talkative.

The first anyone knew of it was in spring. When Orin's stores ran low, Wren filled the basket without being asked. A year went by. By spring nothing had been resolved. Orin had never returned what Wren once gave, and the owing curdled, the way gratitude does when it is carried too long. Soon afterwards. Word reached Wren that Orin had been speaking of the matter behind closed doors. It was Orin who carried the tale. When autumn came again, the story had not ended. Orin and Wren quarreled where everyone could hear, and the village pretended not to listen. A year went by. By winter nothing had been resolved. It fell to Joren — who had seen enough winters to know how grudges end — to sit them at one table. Something mended. Some seasons passed. By the time summer came, the matter had not been resolved. Joren, who had been in the village long enough to remember the last time this had happened, made them speak. Something loosened. Winter came back, and with it the old matter came back too. It fell to Bryn — who had seen enough winters to know how grudges end — to sit them at one table. Something mended. They fought 6 more times. It never seemed to settle anything.

An uneasy peace held between them, thin as spring ice.

## Luma and Tomes: a love

Everyone had come to Luma at least once. Luma was humble and talkative. Everyone had come to Tomes at least once. Tomes was tight-fisted and idle.

The first anyone knew of it was in autumn. When Tomes's stores ran low, Luma filled the basket without being asked. Some seasons passed. By the time spring came, the matter had not been resolved. Luma and Tomes chose each other before the season turned. The village knew before either of them said a word about it. Winter came. Miren was born. The village received them the way it always receives what arrives without asking. By then it was autumn. Miren grew up. The village counted one more among its own. The bread came again, without a word about it.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Tam and Sable: a love

Everyone had come to Sable at least once. Sable was humble and idle.

It began in spring. When Sable's stores ran low, Tam filled the basket without being asked. Soon afterwards. Tam and Sable chose each other before the season turned. The village knew before either of them said a word about it. Summer came. A child — Arvid — came to them. There are things that arrive without ceremony and are felt for years. Spring came. Arvid grew up. The village counted one more among its own. The bread came again, without a word about it.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## After

When the record ends, Tam and Hadwin were as close as two people get in Cold Hollow; Hadwin and Orin still were not speaking.
