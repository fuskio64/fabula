# The Chronicle of Thornby

Thornby held 10 souls at the opening of this record, and it covers 480 days of them. Nothing in it was invented; it was only watched, and told.

18 times, someone in Thornby went hungry rather than ask for help. Pride is its own kind of famine.

When the record closes, 4 favors in Thornby had still not been returned. The village kept those accounts in no book, which is why none of them were ever lost.

One soul in Thornby did not live through these years. A village carries its dead quietly, which is how the dead end up carrying the village.

4 bonds were made in Thornby in this time. Most lasting things begin without anyone marking the moment.

One child was born in Thornby in this time.

## Runa and Pell: a debt unpaid

Everyone had come to Runa at least once. Runa was idle and reserved. Pell, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was tireless and reserved.

It began in winter. Runa went hungry to Pell's door and came back with nothing. A year went by. By winter nothing had been resolved. When Runa's stores ran low, Pell filled the basket without being asked. A year went by. By winter nothing had been resolved. Runa and Pell quarreled where everyone could hear, and the village pretended not to listen. Not many days passed. The favor sat heavy on Runa. It is a hard thing to owe, and Runa took to avoiding Pell's eye. When summer came again, the story had not ended. It fell to Sable — who had seen enough winters to know how grudges end — to sit them at one table. Something mended. Some seasons passed. By the time winter came, the matter had not been resolved. Sable, who had been in the village long enough to remember the last time this had happened, made them speak. Something loosened. And so the fighting went on, season after season; some things need a witness more than a resolution.

The peace that was made did not keep; some cracks want to stay open.

Runa did not outlive this chronicle. A long winter took them before the account closed.

## Wulf and Sable: a love

Everyone had come to Wulf at least once. Wulf was idle and reserved. Everyone had come to Sable at least once. Sable was talkative and tireless.

The first anyone knew of it was in spring. Sable shared bread with Wulf in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. When winter came again, the story had not ended. The bread Sable once gave came back to Sable's own door, and it was Wulf who carried it. Some seasons passed. By the time summer came, the matter had not been resolved. What had grown between Wulf and Sable became something declared. They shared bread and hearth after that. The bread came 5 more times, without ceremony; that is what makes it bread.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.
