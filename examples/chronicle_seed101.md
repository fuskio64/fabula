# What Happened in Saltmere

Saltmere held 10 souls at the opening of this record, and it covers 192 days of them. Nothing in it was invented; it was only watched, and told.

13 times, someone in Saltmere went hungry rather than ask for help. Pride is its own kind of famine.

When the record closes, 8 favors in Saltmere had still not been returned. The village kept those accounts in no book, which is why none of them were ever lost.

3 bonds were made in Saltmere in this time. Most lasting things begin without anyone marking the moment.

## Finn and Bertram: a love

Finn was idle and open-handed. Bertram was proud and reserved.

It began in spring. Bertram shared bread with Finn in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. Some seasons passed. By the time winter came, the matter had not been resolved. The bread Bertram once gave came back to Bertram's own door, and it was Finn who carried it. By then it was spring. Finn and Bertram chose each other before the season turned. The village knew before either of them said a word about it. The bread came 6 more times, without ceremony; that is what makes it bread.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Pell and Coll: a love

Pell, proud and reserved, was known to everyone. Coll was proud and tireless.

It began in winter. When Coll's stores ran low, Pell filled the basket without being asked. Spring came. Coll had not forgotten the old kindness, and when want came to Pell's house, it was returned without a word about it. Some seasons passed. By the time autumn came, the matter had not been resolved. What had grown between Pell and Coll became something declared. They shared bread and hearth after that.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Petra and Linde: a friendship

Petra was talkative and open-handed. Linde, proud and tireless, was known to everyone.

The first anyone knew of it was in winter. When Petra's stores ran low, Linde filled the basket without being asked. When summer came again, the story had not ended. Petra had not forgotten the old kindness, and when want came to Linde's house, it was returned without a word about it. The bread came 3 more times, without ceremony; that is what makes it bread.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.
