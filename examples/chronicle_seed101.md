# What Happened in Saltmere

Saltmere held 10 souls at the opening of this record, and it covers 192 days of them. Nothing in it was invented; it was only watched, and told.

13 times, someone in Saltmere went hungry rather than ask for help. Pride is its own kind of famine.

When the record closes, 8 favors in Saltmere had still not been returned. The village kept those accounts in no book, which is why none of them were ever lost.

3 bonds were made in Saltmere in this time. Most lasting things begin without anyone marking the moment.

## The Village

That winter, most of Saltmere counted its stores by the day. The ovens were careful and the children got the most of what was left.

The spring was generous. Most of Saltmere had more than enough, and surplus food finds its way out the door.

## Finn and Bertram: a love

Finn was idle and open-handed. Bertram was proud and reserved.

The first anyone knew of it was in spring. When Finn's stores ran low, Bertram filled the basket without being asked. Another winter settled in. The cold version of everything returned. The bread Bertram once gave came back to Bertram's own door, and it was Finn who carried it. Spring came. What had grown between Finn and Bertram became something declared. They shared bread and hearth after that. The bread came 6 more times, without ceremony; that is what makes it bread.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Pell and Coll: a love

Pell was proud and reserved. Coll was proud and tireless.

It began in winter. When Coll's stores ran low, Pell filled the basket without being asked. Spring came. Coll had not forgotten the old kindness, and when want came to Pell's house, it was returned without a word about it. When autumn came again, the story had not ended. Pell and Coll chose each other before the season turned. The village knew before either of them said a word about it.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Petra and Linde: a friendship

Petra, talkative and open-handed, was known to everyone. Linde, proud and tireless, was known to everyone.

It began in winter. Linde shared bread with Petra in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. Some seasons passed. By the time summer came, the matter had not been resolved. Petra had not forgotten the old kindness, and when want came to Linde's house, it was returned without a word about it. The bread came 3 more times, without ceremony; that is what makes it bread.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.
