# The Chronicle of Cold Hollow

Cold Hollow held 10 souls at the opening of this record, and it covers 480 days of them. Nothing in it was invented; it was only watched, and told.

29 times, someone in Cold Hollow went hungry rather than ask for help. Pride is its own kind of famine.

When the record closes, 18 favors in Cold Hollow had still not been returned. The village kept those accounts in no book, which is why none of them were ever lost.

One soul in Cold Hollow did not live through these years. A village carries its dead quietly, which is how the dead end up carrying the village.

4 bonds were made in Cold Hollow in this time. Most lasting things begin without anyone marking the moment.

2 children were born in Cold Hollow in this time.

By the close of this record, Cold Hollow counted more souls than it had at the opening.

## Tam and Orin: an unfinished business

Everyone had come to Tam at least once. Tam was talkative and tireless. Orin, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was proud and idle.

It began in autumn. Tam shared bread with Orin in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. A year went by. By winter nothing had been resolved. Tam had once filled Orin's basket; when Tam's own ran empty, Orin's door stayed shut. The village keeps accounts no ledger ever sees, and that one was entered in every head in it. Soon afterwards. The favor sat heavy on Orin. It is a hard thing to owe, and Orin took to avoiding Tam's eye. Some seasons passed. By the time summer came, the matter had not been resolved. It came to hard words between Tam and Orin, in the open street. A year went by. By autumn nothing had been resolved. It fell to Bryn — who had seen enough winters to know how grudges end — to sit them at one table. Something mended. Some seasons passed. By the time spring came, the matter had not been resolved. Bryn, who had been in the village long enough to remember the last time this had happened, made them speak. Something loosened. When autumn came again, the story had not ended. It fell to Bryn — who had seen enough winters to know how grudges end — to sit them at one table. Something mended. Winter came. Tam turned Orin away empty-handed, and the whole village felt colder for it. Not many days passed. And so the fighting went on, season after season; some things need a witness more than a resolution.

An uneasy peace held between them, thin as spring ice.

## Luma and Tomes: a love

Everyone had come to Luma at least once. Luma was humble and talkative. Everyone had come to Tomes at least once. Tomes was tight-fisted and idle.

It began in autumn. When Tomes's stores ran low, Luma filled the basket without being asked. When spring came again, the story had not ended. Luma and Tomes chose each other before the season turned. The village knew before either of them said a word about it. Winter came. A child — Miren — came to them. There are things that arrive without ceremony and are felt for years. Autumn came. Miren grew up. The village counted one more among its own. The bread came again, without a word about it.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Leif and Joren: a friendship

Leif was idle and tight-fisted. Everyone had come to Joren at least once. Joren was talkative and tight-fisted.

The first anyone knew of it was in spring. When Leif's stores ran low, Joren filled the basket without being asked. Summer came. The bread Joren once gave came back to Joren's own door, and it was Leif who carried it. Autumn came. Joren turned Leif away empty-handed, and the whole village felt colder for it. The bread came 6 more times, without ceremony; that is what makes it bread.

Whatever had been broken, they mended it better than new.

Leif did not outlive this chronicle. A long winter took them before the account closed.
