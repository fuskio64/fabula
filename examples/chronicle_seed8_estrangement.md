# The Chronicle of Dunmore

Dunmore held 10 souls at the opening of this record, and it covers 384 days of them. Nothing in it was invented; it was only watched, and told.

9 times, someone in Dunmore went hungry rather than ask for help. Pride is its own kind of famine.

When the record closes, 6 favors in Dunmore had still not been returned. The village kept those accounts in no book, which is why none of them were ever lost.

One soul in Dunmore did not live through these years. A village carries its dead quietly, which is how the dead end up carrying the village.

3 bonds were made in Dunmore in this time. Most lasting things begin without anyone marking the moment.

2 children were born in Dunmore in this time.

By the close of this record, Dunmore counted more souls than it had at the opening.

## The Village

The winter was a hungry one. More than half the houses in Dunmore had lean pantries, and no one pretended otherwise.

That was not the only lean season. Dunmore counted 4 in the course of these years — hunger was a recurring visitor, not a single hard winter.

The spring was generous. Most of Dunmore had more than enough, and surplus food finds its way out the door.

## Dara and Rafe: a feud

Dara, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was reserved and tireless. Everyone had come to Rafe at least once. Rafe was tight-fisted and idle.

The first anyone knew of it was in winter. Rafe went hungry to Dara's door and came back with nothing. Spring came. It came to hard words between Dara and Rafe, in the open street. A year went by. By winter nothing had been resolved. Whatever the original quarrel had been, what Dara and Rafe had between them had passed from bitter into permanent. And so the fighting went on, season after season; some things need a witness more than a resolution.

No one mended it. By the end they passed each other without a word.

## Dara and Edwin: an estrangement

Everyone had come to Edwin at least once. Edwin was open-handed and tireless.

It began in winter. Dara turned Edwin away empty-handed, and the whole village felt colder for it. Some seasons passed. By the time autumn came, the matter had not been resolved. Dara and Edwin quarreled where everyone could hear, and the village pretended not to listen. By then it was winter. Dara shared bread with Edwin in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. By then it was spring. Edwin had not forgotten the old kindness, and when want came to Dara's house, it was returned without a word about it. And so the fighting went on, season after season; some things need a witness more than a resolution.

No one mended it. By the end they passed each other without a word.

## Sable and Pell: a debt unpaid

Sable, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was open-handed and tireless. Everyone had come to Pell at least once. Pell was tight-fisted and proud.

The first anyone knew of it was in winter. When Pell's stores ran low, Sable filled the basket without being asked. A year went by. By winter nothing had been resolved. Pell had never returned what Sable once gave, and the owing curdled, the way gratitude does when it is carried too long. By then it was spring. It came to hard words between Sable and Pell, in the open street. They fought 3 more times. It never seemed to settle anything.

An uneasy peace held between them, thin as spring ice.

Pell did not outlive this chronicle; a sudden blow, when there was nothing left to absorb it.

## Sable and Elsa: a love

Everyone had come to Elsa at least once. Elsa was humble and tight-fisted.

It began in summer. What had grown between Sable and Elsa became something declared. They shared bread and hearth after that. By then it was winter. A child — Tomes — came to them. There are things that arrive without ceremony and are felt for years.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## After

Pell had kept everything balanced to the last. A complicated kind of person to lose.

At the close of this record, Runa was the person most others in Dunmore kept near — the village's center, in whatever way villages have centers. Dara was present in Dunmore through all of this, but not truly woven into it. There, but at the village's edge.

In the end, Eskel and Runa were still finding reasons to cross each other's paths in Dunmore. Dara and Rafe were still finding reasons not to.
