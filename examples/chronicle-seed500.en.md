# The Chronicle of Netherfen

Netherfen held 8 souls at the opening of this record, and it covers 768 days of them. Nothing in it was invented; it was only watched, and told.

17 times, someone in Netherfen went hungry rather than ask for help. Pride is its own kind of famine.

When the record closes, 17 favors in Netherfen had still not been returned. The village kept those accounts in no book, which is why none of them were ever lost.

5 bonds were made in Netherfen in this time. Most lasting things begin without anyone marking the moment.

4 children were born in Netherfen in this time.

By the close of this record, Netherfen counted more souls than it had at the opening.

## Arvid and Yse: a debt unpaid

Arvid, tireless and talkative, was known to everyone. Yse, talkative and idle, was known to everyone.

It began in winter. When Yse's stores ran low, Arvid filled the basket without being asked. Spring came. Yse had never returned what Arvid once gave, and the owing curdled, the way gratitude does when it is carried too long. Summer came. Word reached Arvid that Yse had been speaking of the matter behind closed doors. It was Luma who carried the tale. Winter came. It came to hard words between Arvid and Yse, in the open street. Spring came. Arvid had once filled Yse's basket; when Arvid's own ran empty, Yse's door stayed shut. The village keeps accounts no ledger ever sees, and that one was entered in every head in it. Not many days passed. Luma sat them both at one table and would not let either leave angry. Something loosened. By then it was spring. Rafe sat them both at one table and would not let either leave angry. Something loosened. By then it was spring. It took Rafe, whom they both trusted, to make them speak again. By then it was autumn. It took Rafe, whom they both trusted, to make them speak again. And so it went on, season after season, until counting lost its point.

The peace that was made did not keep; some cracks want to stay open.

## Luma and Sorel: a debt unpaid

Luma was tireless and proud. Sorel, open-handed and proud, was known to everyone.

It began in spring. Luma shared bread with Sorel in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. By then it was summer. The favor sat heavy on Sorel. It is a hard thing to owe, and Sorel took to avoiding Luma's eye. By then it was autumn. Someone let Luma know what Sorel had been saying. It was Sorel who carried the tale. By then it was winter. It came to hard words between Luma and Sorel, in the open street. Spring came. It took Dara, whom they both trusted, to make them speak again. And so it went on, season after season, until counting lost its point.

The peace that was made did not keep; some cracks want to stay open.

## Luma and Yse: a love

It began in spring. Luma shared bread with Yse in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. Summer came. Luma and Yse chose each other before the season turned. The village knew before either of them said a word about it. Summer came. It came to hard words between Luma and Yse, in the open street. And so it went on, season after season, until counting lost its point.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Rafe and Mara: a friendship

Mara was humble and reserved.

It began in spring. Rafe shared bread with Mara in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. Soon afterwards. Rafe turned Mara away empty-handed, and the whole village felt colder for it. Winter came. The bread Rafe once gave came back to Rafe's own door, and it was Mara who carried it. And so it went on, season after season; some things do not need remarking to continue.

Whatever had been broken, they mended it better than new.

## Dara and Asha: a love

Dara was talkative and humble. Asha was tireless and reserved.

The first anyone knew of it was in winter. Dara went hungry to Asha's door and came back with nothing. Soon afterwards. When Dara's stores ran low, Asha filled the basket without being asked. By then it was spring. Dara had not forgotten the old kindness, and when want came to Asha's house, it was returned without a word about it. Soon afterwards. What had grown between Dara and Asha became something declared. They shared bread and hearth after that. And it happened 3 more times, without anyone making a fuss of it; kindness keeps a calendar too.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Bryn and Eskel: a love

Bryn, who grew up here, was tireless and tight-fisted. Eskel, who grew up here, was reserved and tight-fisted.

It began in spring. Bryn shared bread with Eskel in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. Winter came. What had grown between Bryn and Eskel became something declared. They shared bread and hearth after that.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

When the record ends, Arvid and Dara were as close as two people get in Netherfen; Asha and Sorel still were not speaking.
