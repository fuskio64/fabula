# The Chronicle of Netherfen

Netherfen held 10 souls at the opening of this record, and it covers 480 days of them. Nothing in it was invented; it was only watched, and told.

26 times, someone in Netherfen went hungry rather than ask for help. Pride is its own kind of famine.

When the record closes, 23 favors in Netherfen had still not been returned. The village kept those accounts in no book, which is why none of them were ever lost.

One soul in Netherfen did not live through these years. A village carries its dead quietly, which is how the dead end up carrying the village.

5 bonds were made in Netherfen in this time. Most lasting things begin without anyone marking the moment.

One child was born in Netherfen in this time.

## Rafe and Sorel: a debt unpaid

Everyone had come to Rafe at least once. Rafe was proud and tireless. Everyone had come to Sorel at least once. Sorel was open-handed and proud.

It began in spring. When Sorel's stores ran low, Rafe filled the basket without being asked. 2 years had passed. By autumn the village was still carrying it. The favor sat heavy on Sorel. It is a hard thing to owe, and Sorel took to avoiding Rafe's eye. Soon afterwards. Word reached Rafe that Sorel had been speaking of the matter behind closed doors. It was Sorel who carried the tale. Winter came. Rafe and Sorel quarreled where everyone could hear, and the village pretended not to listen. And so the fighting went on, season after season; some things need a witness more than a resolution.

No one mended it. By the end they passed each other without a word.

## Asha and Petra: a love

Everyone had come to Asha at least once. Asha was tireless and reserved. Petra, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was talkative and idle.

It began in spring. When Petra's stores ran low, Asha filled the basket without being asked. Not many days passed. The bread Asha once gave came back to Asha's own door, and it was Petra who carried it. Summer came. What had grown between Asha and Petra became something declared. They shared bread and hearth after that. By then it was summer. Gust was born. The village received them the way it always receives what arrives without asking. By then it was spring. Gust grew up. The village counted one more among its own.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Asha and Sorel: a debt unpaid

It began in winter. Asha shared bread with Sorel in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. A year went by. By summer nothing had been resolved. The favor sat heavy on Sorel. It is a hard thing to owe, and Sorel took to avoiding Asha's eye. When winter came again, the story had not ended. It came to hard words between Asha and Sorel, in the open street. They fought 5 more times. It never seemed to settle anything.

No one mended it. By the end they passed each other without a word.

## Rafe and Mara: a friendship

Mara, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was humble and reserved.

The first anyone knew of it was in winter. Rafe shared bread with Mara in a lean stretch, and it was not forgotten. Soon afterwards. The bread Rafe once gave came back to Rafe's own door, and it was Mara who carried it. And so it went on, season after season; some things do not need remarking to continue.

They were lucky in each other, and they knew it.

## Arvid and Luma: a love

Arvid, who had been here longest and remembered what others had forgotten, was tireless and talkative. Everyone had come to Luma at least once. Luma was tireless and proud.

It began in winter. Arvid and Luma chose each other before the season turned. The village knew before either of them said a word about it. A year went by. By winter nothing had been resolved. Luma outlived Arvid. The village did not leave Luma alone with it, which is how grief is carried in a place like that.

They had been lucky in each other. The ending was a sad one, but it was a sadness the village recognized.

Arvid did not outlive this chronicle; a sudden blow, when there was nothing left to absorb it. They left behind Luma.

When the record ends, Dara and Luma were as close as two people get in Netherfen; Rafe and Sorel still were not speaking.
